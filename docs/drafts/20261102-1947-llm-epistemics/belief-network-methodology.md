# Consequence-led belief workflow — methodology v0.1

Status: **proposed operating method with an initial cooperative prototype**.
After the partial RTS framing case (J23–J24), the user explicitly requested the
[portable playbook and bookkeeping CLI](../../../scripts/belief-workflow/README.md)
(J26). This is not a validated end-to-end procedure, installed skill, trusted
ratification service or authorization to construct the RTS. It operationalizes
[joint construction](belief-network-construction.md) and the
[validation frame](belief-network-validation.md); the earlier
[DAG design](belief-dag-design.md) remains a source of proposals, not existing tooling.

## 1. What this case contributes

| Observed in the framing case | What it does not establish |
|---|---|
| The human requested ordinary chat rather than the pending forms; eight scoped confirmations were recorded. | Chat is generally superior, eight independent trials occurred, or answering “yes” demonstrates complete intent capture. |
| Consequential scope was clarified: mode/objective, combat domain, factions, economy, roles, information, AI fairness and browser experience. | All of these were pre-existing intentions rather than intentions formed or revised during discussion. |
| Five initially agent-proposed goal IDs were retained while their content was clarified. | The human independently chose the topology, or materially different decompositions and omitted outcomes were considered exhaustively. |
| The living document mixed outcomes, history and proposals; it was compacted while preserving the prior draft separately. | Fewer lines or nodes reduce cognitive load without losing meaning. |
| The same answers had to be propagated across the network, README, inventory and local state. | A generated projection would be semantically correct; it would address duplication, not interpretation itself. |
| No attention ceiling was set beforehand, and no active-human-time measure exists. | The initial framing was budget-controlled, or a later cap can repair that omission. |
| No game has been implemented or accepted; private acceptance cases remain unknown. | Belief-guided construction, delivery judgment, continuing usability or reduced overload works. |

This is evidence of **a worked agent-led clarification and compaction episode**.
The methodology below deliberately includes steps the episode has not exercised.

## 2. Governing distinctions

**Compress implementation, not unresolved intent.** Discuss a choice when plausible
alternatives would change an intended outcome, applicability, accepted risk or
consequential side effect. Do not ask again when existing intent decides it, or
ask the human to establish a fact the agent can investigate.

Keep separate:

- **Human authority:** what the person actually requested, clarified or deliberately
  revised, with the exact antecedent of a scoped answer.
- **Agent interpretation:** the compiled goal structure, inferred supporting
  obligations and delegated design. A faithful paraphrase is a claim to check,
  not another human act.
- **Factual warrant:** observed behavior, environmental assumptions and missing
  evidence. Agreement can require a guarantee or accept a risk; it cannot make
  a descriptive premise true.
- **Implementation authorization:** permission for a stage and its side effects.
  Agreeing an outcome does not itself authorize installation, deployment or push.

Positive accomplishments matter alongside prohibitions. An inert AI can obey
fairness rules without providing opposition. Examples distinguish interpretations;
they do not replace the broader goal or authorize exceptions in unlisted cases.

## 3. Operating loop

These are moves to use as needed, not a questionnaire every task must complete.
Already-scoped work may reuse an existing baseline with little or no new dialogue.

### A. Anchor the task and operating agreement

Preserve the request, relevant existing agreements, current authorization and
known boundaries. Identify who will use the delivered result and judge it.
Agree the interaction channel. For a study, prospectively agree an attention
policy and measurement method, including an explicit no-hard-cap option.
Record missing preconditions as missing; do not infer active time from timestamps.

Separate study conditions from product requirements. An unbudgeted case may yield
observations, but cannot be reported as a budget-controlled trial. A research
precondition also must not silently become a new product feature.

### B. Propose and challenge a small acceptance model

Draft goals in the human's vocabulary, with relevant conditions, exclusions,
known obstacles and consequential assumptions. A domain inventory can support the
agent's thinking; it is not automatically the human's review workload.

Challenge both interpretations **and the proposed grouping itself**:

- Could two systems satisfy the summary yet deserve different acceptance decisions?
- Could every example pass while the positive outcome is missing?
- What important observer, failure, condition, dependency or side effect is absent?
- Which issue can existing intent or new factual investigation settle without asking?

A fresh reader, domain checklist or another family can propose challenges. None
certifies completeness. Select the next question by consequence, uncertainty and
irreversibility, not by completing a fixed number of graph fields.

### C. Resolve one consequential difference

Use the person's chosen channel. Present a short observable contrast or coherent
proposal, label the recommendation, and leave room for “neither,” correction or
deferral. One focused yes/no question can be useful; an uninterrupted sequence of
such questions must not be mistaken for evidence against unoffered alternatives.

Do not bundle unrelated material choices merely to obtain one approval. Do not
fragment a coherent outcome into library or function-signature decisions either.
Occasionally invite additions, removals or regrouping of goals. When the study
holds private cases back, do not use this invitation to solicit those cases.

A topic change is not an answer to an old question; it can be a new request or
correction in its own right. An unanswered default stays proposed. Ambiguity blocks
only work whose authorization materially depends on that distinction; an unnecessary
engineering question does not revoke discretion already delegated. Independent
work may continue. Do not poll or repeat the same unanswered question.

### D. Preserve the act; update the view

Retain the original answer and the proposal/context it addressed. Record what it
settles, what it leaves open, and any supplied qualifications or reasons. A “yes”
to fog rules does not approve AI resource bonuses or the entire feature inventory.

Keep the raw human act distinct from the agent's interpretation of its scope.
Retain the interpretation's author, revision and source references. Revising it
cannot create, expand or supersede a human act; even a model-assigned “direct clause”
label is not mechanically verified approval. Do not turn rich or ambiguous replies
into machine-approved clause sets by fiat.

Supersession/retraction identifies what is superseded or retracted. Incompatible
acts without an applicable explicit resolution remain visible; append order or
timestamps alone do not establish revised intent.

Update a compact current projection rather than appending every exchange to it.
History, inventories and implementation detail remain inspectable elsewhere.
Known material conditions must stay visible; neither a row cap nor an anonymous
overflow count may hide them. Five goals happened to be used here, not proven optimal.

### E. Challenge the compilation and close an active scope

Show the compact outcome view, its known open boundaries and the delegation
boundary. Give the human an opportunity to change the agent's decomposition or
compiled interpretation—not only endorse polished wording.

Prior scoped confirmations remain valid; this is not ritual re-ratification of
every word. Newly introduced or materially changed meanings are not covered by
those confirmations. Record exactly what the closure covers. An opportunity to
object, without a reply, does not approve new meaning or settle an open interpretation.

Proposed readiness:

- Known material conflicts/choices are resolved, or affected scope is explicitly
  deferred without discarding cross-scope obligations.
- Important descriptive assumptions have warrant or visible uncertainty and an
  investigation/validation route. Planned implementation evidence may still be
  pending; human approval is not factual verification. A known limitation that
  materially changes applicability, risk or the eventual acceptance basis needs an
  explicit scope/risk decision, not merely an “unknown” label.
- Broad outcomes retain their positive obligations and a stated evidence route.
  An impractical material check is a visible gap needing an explicit acceptance-
  basis decision, not permission to shrink the goal or demand proof before coding.
- The baseline is identifiable, routine engineering discretion is delegated, and
  the user has authorized the construction stage under these conditions.

Record the applicable execution-question policy; existing instructions may already
settle it. Proposed default: pause the affected branch and ask. Queuing until a
review point or deferring a branch requires an already-authorized arrangement.
Rescheduling work does not exclude a required outcome from the final delivery.

This is **scoped readiness**, not proof of complete elicitation. Unknown omissions
remain possible. Cumulative human effort matters even if every exchange is short.

### F. Construct under the baseline; reopen only what needs reopening

The agent owns refinement, specification, implementation and detailed checks inside
the delegated envelope. Internal milestones do not redefine the final deliverable.

When something conflicts, distinguish:

| Finding | Appropriate response |
|---|---|
| Implementation contradicts an agreed outcome | Reproduce and repair the implementation; do not ask the human to re-ratify the same goal. |
| Evidence is absent or a factual premise is uncertain | Investigate; report the uncertainty and checked scope. |
| Intent has materially different unresolved interpretations | Ask a consequential question or explicitly defer affected work. |
| A real constraint appears to make the current refinement unsuitable | Seek alternatives preserving the goal before proposing an intent change. |
| The human deliberately changes the outcome | Record the new act, what it supersedes, and affected evidence; do not call it original-intent success. |

Apply the agreed interruption/deferment policy to affected work; continue unrelated
authorized work. A new agent default cannot acquire human authority through a generated spec, passing
test, edited baseline file or repeated report.

### G. Review delivery in three directions

1. **Intent → network:** did the framing/compilation omit or distort an intended
   acceptance distinction? Human-held cases may be introduced at the agreed
   delivery stage; do not assume they were precommitted unless they actually were.
2. **Network → product:** what observed public behavior, complete workflows and
   inspectable evidence support, contradict or leave each outcome unchecked?
3. **Product → agreed basis:** what consequential behavior, dependency, side effect,
   exclusion or shortcut appeared without an agreed or delegated basis?

Bind results to the actual artifact and checked conditions. Report failing,
skipped, unavailable and unchecked cases without filtering them into a green
summary. A trace link is accountability, not semantic entailment; a model review
is attributed judgment, not an independent human-intent oracle.

The human gets an outcome-level assessment, material departures, open questions
and a route to targeted evidence—not the whole generated specification. Acceptance,
repair, deliberate intent revision and deferral are different outcomes. Record
unfinished work and total human effort with their measurement limitations.

## 4. Artifacts: logical records, not six new manuals

| Record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Original context and proposal snapshots | Preserve the request and what was actually presented, including scope and revision. Retain content, not hashes alone. |
| Scoped human acts and interpretation provenance | Separate actual input from compiled meanings; retain supersession and unresolved conflicts. |
| Compact current view | Goals, consequential conditions, open obstacles/choices and delegated scope, with short source/evidence paths. |
| Agent refinement and evidence manifest | Detailed obligations, design choices, checks and raw artifacts; normally hidden but inspectable. |
| Delivery assessment | Per-goal checked/contradicted/unknown scope, departures and consequential untraced behavior. |
| Study accounting, when applicable | Consent, prospective attention policy, measurement method, missing data and protocol deviations; separate from outcome text. |

A manual case can use Markdown and existing test reports. These records should
be generated from shared sources where possible, not maintained as competing
copies of authority. Every record need not be its own file or service.

## 5. Components: semantic playbooks versus mechanical code

### Skills/playbooks

Start with **one consequence-led co-framing playbook**, covering A–E and a small
handoff/reopening section. Trigger it for user-requested joint framing of a large
or underspecified delegated task—not every defect fix or fully specified change.
It owns materiality judgments, contrast selection, source interpretation,
compaction challenges and closure. It cannot enforce authority or prove omissions absent.

Keep **baseline-bound execution/reopening** and **evidence-based acceptance review**
as phase modules initially. Split them into separate skills only when actual use
shows separate reusable workflows. The current case has not exercised them.

Reuse `constructive-test-taxonomy` for check design and `dual-tests` for external
adapters rather than copying those procedures. The existing
`agent-behaviour-pilot` draft concerns controlled agent-subject experiments and
explicitly excludes human-participant pilots; it is not this operational method.

At J25 these were component specifications, not a reusable skill established by
recurrence. In J26 the user explicitly requested provisional implementation:
a portable SKILL.md and limited checker/projector now exist, **not installed or
promoted as validated practice**. The new authorship request does not turn the
partial case into efficacy evidence. Trusted host capture, storage services and
end-to-end human acceptance remain outside the implemented prototype.

### Code boundaries

| Component | Mechanical responsibility | What remains outside its claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Pure state/projection core** | Stable IDs, kinds, source references, revision relationships, legal event transitions and deterministic current/delta views. Separate normative provenance from factual/evidence state. | Materiality, faithful paraphrase, complete question selection or refinement entailment. |
| **Proposal/act store** | Retain exact presentation, structured scope and revision snapshots, initially in existing files if sufficient. If an append interface is introduced, define expected-revision conflict checks and explicit failure/durability behavior. | That a caller or string labelled “human” actually is the human; a new storage abstraction is not mandatory. |
| **Thin host adapter** | Preserve real input, including unsolicited requests/corrections with no proposal reference. Bind a claimed approval to its antecedent; obtain trustworthy origin/reply metadata where the host supplies it. | Authentic input origin does not establish the intended antecedent or covered scope. Without explicit reply/selection metadata, those are attributed interpretations. No particular SDK integration is selected yet. |
| **Evidence binding/checker** | Link claim/baseline revisions, scope, code/artifact identity, declared dependencies, check/toolchain/environment, oracle provenance, outcomes and raw artifact digests; flag mismatches and recorded stale dependencies. | That passing linked checks prove a broad goal, oracle correctness, enjoyment, or absence of untraced consequences. |

These are boundaries, not a prescription for four packages or servers. A small
library plus a command-line adapter may suffice for cooperative bookkeeping.
Use existing build/test reports before inventing another runner.

Minimum conceptual records are a **proposal snapshot**, **human input/act**,
**claim interpretation with source and derivation**, and **evidence manifest**.
Only claimed scoped approval requires an antecedent; new human instructions need
not answer a proposal. Keep raw input separate from interpreted approval scope.

Define canonicalization and binding before implementation; retain exact text and
scenario strings rather than collapsing potentially meaningful whitespace. Claimed scoped approvals bind immutable
proposal versions; unsolicited instructions retain their immutable source input and
relevant context without requiring a proposal reference. A baseline selects claim
revisions. Evidence
binds that baseline or relevant immutable claims, the actual checked artifact and
its declared dependencies, including applicability conditions and check revisions.
A mutable branch name, or a commit hiding unrecorded workspace changes, is not
sufficient. CAS orders writes; it does not replace content binding. Editing an
approved presentation/scope must not silently carry its old approval forward.

Structural checks may report missing references, old proposal revisions or
mismatched evidence subjects. Do **not** derive a semantic “supported” badge merely
because every generated leaf has a passing linked test. Independence is a profile
of oracle, author and method, not an executor label or a universal strength ladder.

### Authority boundary: a necessary limitation

A skill is instructions, not enforcement. A ledger, human-invoked CLI, hash chain
or pre-commit hook is only a cooperative control when the agent can invoke or
rewrite it under the same account. Hashes establish byte identity against a
trusted reference; they do not authenticate a person or preserve meaning [L10].

Before claiming exclusive human ratification, a host adapter must derive the act
from an independently controlled real user-input path, and the agent must not
be able to forge that path or rewrite its authority store. The precise boundary
needs separate design and verification. Strong hostile-agent isolation is not
part of this first prototype; label the actual assurance rather than implying it.

### Existing code inspected

The historical `epistemics-pilots/human/ratify_session.py` is a self-contained
standard-library P-D instrument: synthetic batches, accept/reject/unsure,
response timing, optional notes, NASA-TLX and JSONL output containing experimental
truth labels [L9]. It has no immutable presented-proposal snapshot, content/version
binding, authority identity, CAS store, current-view projection or evidence manifest.

Reuse its interaction ideas selectively, **not its log as an authority ledger**.
Its timer does not measure all human effort. Do not bring answer-key/planted-item
machinery into ordinary framing, or revive the rejected workload instrument by
renaming it. In this reuse inspection, neither its fixtures nor private
participant data were inspected.

## 6. Verification for a future prototype

Prefer public-contract Behavioral-Active-Blackbox Atomic/Group checks for the
pure core. Record test origin and normative source separately; “specified” does
not mean human-authored or human-approved. If a storage interface is introduced,
run its public contract against an in-memory implementation and the real storage
backend; add targeted backend tests for the exact guarantees actually promised.
Dual tests do not justify creating an otherwise unnecessary interface.

Mechanical tests check preservation, explicit provenance/binding, refusal to apply
missing or untrusted scope, and supplied interpretation states. Semantic evaluation
separately challenges whether real replies were interpreted faithfully; fixture
passes do not establish general ambiguity or topic-change detection.

The [prototype suite](../../../scripts/belief-workflow/README.md#validation) in J26
executes only implemented cooperative CLI contracts. Host/semantic validation and
unimplemented storage guarantees below remain proposed, not passed.

Challenges for the corresponding implemented responsibilities include:

- Partial approvals, unrelated confirmations, ambiguous/qualified replies and
  topic changes; no accidental approval of neighboring or older proposals.
- Changed presentation/content, wrong revision, duplicate events, supersession and
  missing provenance. Changing an interpretation cannot retroactively change the
  proposal answered; changing a requirement/applicability condition with unchanged
  code must not silently reuse evidence for the new claim.
- Deterministic replay, dangling references and invalid cycles for the relations
  actually required to be acyclic—not a universal ban on all network relations.
- Wrong artifact/check/environment binding; skipped checks, failed controls and
  incomplete evidence preserved as such. Unchanged valid inputs may reuse clearly
  labelled cached evidence only when their dependency binding is established.
- If append/CAS is implemented: a stale write applies none of its batch; a successful
  append exposes the whole batch to replay; duplicate-event behavior is defined.
  Test interruption/corruption on the real storage backend against its declared
  failure model. Never silently project a clean authoritative state from damaged
  input. Acknowledged durability is bounded by that model, not proven by a dummy.
- The real input adapter's source/reply binding. Tests must distinguish a model
  supplying “human: yes” from a genuine trusted input event where that guarantee
  is claimed; scripts cannot prove semantic equivalence of natural-language scope.

Negative controls must actually violate the property being checked. Generated
scorer cases can share the generator's omissions; review real traces and retain
failures. This is the broader proposed test plan; J26's software results do not
establish any unimplemented host, semantic, storage or human-workflow guarantee.

## 7. Order of work and research checks

1. Use this document as a **manual provisional playbook**; do not install a large
   skill suite or build a graph UI first.
2. Exercise the untried baseline handoff, construction and delivery review. Finish
   the RTS under its agreed final scope, or explicitly authorize a separate smaller
   task; a smaller task must not masquerade as completion of the RTS.
3. Automate demonstrated bookkeeping: snapshots, acts, projections and binding.
   Add the host-specific trust boundary before making stronger authority claims.
4. Only then justify separate skill packaging, evidence adapters or richer UI from
   actual repeated work. Code and semantic evaluation can iterate; code need not
   wait for an efficacy claim, but does not create that claim.

For evaluation, distinguish **faithful framing**, **conforming implementation**,
**discrimination at acceptance**, **uncovered consequences**, **human effort** and
**later usability**. Use independent/held-out cases where available and preserve
clarification versus deliberate change versus unknown history. No agreed validated
mechanical attention/overload measure exists in this case (Q21); do not replace
that gap with time, question counts or view size. Any future measurement needs
prospective consent, disclosed limits and accounting for its own burden. Qualitative
judgments can be recorded as such, not renamed quantitative overload evidence.
Include false acceptance, false rejection, unfinished work and unchecked scope.

One completed case would still establish only feasibility. Fewer questions,
smaller views, unanimous reviewers or green tests do not establish reduced
overload. The unresolved core remains: **how to select consequential contrasts
without inheriting the generator's omissions or making the human review a full spec.**
